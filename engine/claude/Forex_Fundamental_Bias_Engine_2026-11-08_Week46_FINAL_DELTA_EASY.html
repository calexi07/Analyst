1 · How to read a score

Each currency gets a score from −18 to +18. +3 or more = bullish (fundamentals support it), −3 or less = bearish, in between = neutral. A pair's lean comes from the gap between its two currencies: a gap of 3 or more gives a lean.

2 · The week in short

  • The risk mood turned 'afraid': US shares fell about 5.6% in four weeks, the fear index (VIX) is above 25 and risky company bonds got more expensive. Safe currencies (USD, JPY, CHF) and gold gain; AUD and NZD lose.
  • USD is strongest (+7) after very strong jobs data. CAD is weakest (−6): oil fell, jobs fell and the US tariff stays. NZD is −3 after the final check.
  • AUD drops from +5 to 0 and GBP from +4 to −3: both central banks hiked but sounded less keen on more.
  • Gold scores +14: fear, plus strong buying by central banks and funds.
  • Main event: US inflation (CPI), Tue 10 Nov 15:30. No big central-bank decision this week.

16 · Glossary

Bullish / bearish
Fundamentals support / weigh on the currency.
Carry
How much interest a currency pays compared with others.
Hawkish / dovish
Wants higher / lower interest rates.
Priced
What the market already expects.
Risk mood
Whether investors are calm or afraid.
Safe haven
A currency or asset people buy when afraid (USD, JPY, CHF, gold).
Spread
The extra interest one borrower pays over another.
bp
Basis point = 0.01 percentage point.
Final check
The short check before Monday that can correct a score only for new news or a missed fact.
Hysteresis
A rule that keeps a score unchanged when the new calculation differs by less than 1 point.
Conviction
How clear the lean is (low / medium / high).
