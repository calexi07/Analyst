1 · The answer: did the forecast work?

Mixed. The ranking of currencies did not work this week: the match score was −0.12 (0 = no relation, +1 = perfect). But pairs did better: of the 18 pairs with a direction, 7 worked, 3 failed and 8 barely moved.

  • Main reason: two central banks surprised after the forecast was written — New Zealand did not hike (NZD fell) and Canada did hike (CAD rose).
  • What worked: the weak-euro view. Most EUR pairs moved the right way (EURUSD, EURGBP, EURCHF, EURCAD, EURAUD).
  • The only high-conviction pair, EURAUD, worked — but only just (−0.30%).
  • Gold fell about 3% over the week (−3.62% on Friday alone), even though markets were nervous. That is the second week in a row gold did not act as a safe haven.
  • One week proves nothing. Nothing in the method changes until 26 weeks of results exist. This was week 1 of 26.

2 · How to read the results

Ranking match (ρ, "rho")
Compares the model's order of the 8 currencies with the order of their real moves. +1 = same order, 0 = no relation, −1 = opposite order.
σ ("sigma", normal move size)
How big a weekly move normally is. For now a fixed size is used: 1.2% for most pairs, 0.8% for EURGBP and EURCHF, 1.8% for GBPJPY (marked "uncalibrated" in the technical report). After 26 weeks of data, real sizes will be measured.
Worked / failed / barely moved
Worked = the pair moved in the forecast direction. Failed = it moved the other way. Barely moved = it moved less than a quarter of a normal move (0.25σ), so it does not count either way.
Why one week proves nothing
With 8 currencies, a good or bad week can easily be luck. The method needs about 26 weeks before its results mean anything.

5 · Why the misses happened

Every big miss gets one code. Here is what each code means in plain words:

CodeMeaning
N1New news after the forecast (nobody could know it).
MMissed: the information existed before the forecast, but the research did not find it.
N2A known event was given too much or too little weight.
IInterpretation error: the facts were right, the reading was wrong.
PAlready priced: the market already expected it, or expectations moved the other way.
FMoney flows / positioning: traders buying or selling for reasons other than fundamentals.
XNoise: a small move, not worth explaining.
DData problem.
  • NZD → N1. Surprise RBNZ hold on 28 Oct. Caused the EURNZD miss.
  • CAD → N1. BoC hike on 28 Oct with "more may be needed". Caused the CADJPY miss. CAD was strong two weeks in a row while scoring −2: this pattern is now being counted.
  • JPY → P / F. The hawkish BoJ was not believed, and early-week risk appetite sold the yen. Caused the CHFJPY miss.
  • AUD → P. Inflation in line, hike partly expected already: no new push.
  • GBP → N1. Calm budget; the risk the model feared did not happen.
  • No "M" this week: the research did not miss any information that existed before the forecast.

6 · Gold

Forecast: gold score +3, but XAUUSD gap +1 → two-sided (no direction), so it is not scored.

What happened: gold fell about −2.96% over the week, almost all on Friday (−3.62% in one day), during a tech-led stock sell-off and after hot US inflation (PCE) data.

Why it matters: gold is supposed to rise when markets are afraid. For the second week running it fell on a fearful day. Possible reasons: selling gold to cover losses elsewhere, or higher US yields. This is not yet explained and stays on the watch list.

8 · Data quality

The Daily Observer export was complete: 29 instruments × 5 days, no duplicates, correct week. One small issue: prices are logged with 2 decimals, so cross-checks between pairs can be off by up to about 0.08% (the test allows 0.05%). This is a rounding effect, not an error. Tip: 3–4 decimals or real closing prices in the export would fix it.

9 · Lessons and patterns to watch

No rule changes until 26 weeks. These are only notes to check over time:

  • Big meetings inside the week are risky. When a central-bank decision falls inside the forecast week, the score can look "already priced" but a surprise can still move the currency a lot (RBNZ, BoC this week).
  • Safe havens did not act like safe havens. On fearful days CHF and gold did not rise as expected. To be reviewed at 26 weeks.
  • CAD strong while scored negative — two weeks in a row. If the same reason repeats 4 times in 12 weeks, a method change can be proposed.
  • No warning triggered: no currency moved strongly against its forecast three weeks in a row.

11 · Glossary

Attribution code
A one-letter label explaining why a forecast missed (section 5).
Central bank
The institution that sets a country's interest rate.
Currency strength
A currency's average move against the other seven during the week.
ETF
A fund that holds gold; its buying or selling moves the gold price.
Hawkish / dovish
Hawkish = wants higher rates (usually good for the currency). Dovish = wants lower rates.
Hike / hold
Raise / keep the interest rate.
PCE
The US inflation measure the Fed prefers.
Priced / already priced
What the market already expects. If something is already priced, it usually moves the currency less.
Risk-on / risk-off
Risk-on = investors are confident and buy riskier assets. Risk-off = investors are afraid and buy safe havens.
Safe haven
An asset people buy when afraid: traditionally JPY, CHF and gold.
Two-sided
A pair with no clear fundamental direction; not scored.
Uncalibrated
Using fixed move sizes until enough real data exist.
