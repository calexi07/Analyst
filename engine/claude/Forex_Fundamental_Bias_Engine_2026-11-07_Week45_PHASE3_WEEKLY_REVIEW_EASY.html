1 · The answer: did the forecast work?

No. The ranking of currencies came out almost upside down: the match score was -0.47 (0 = no relation, +1 = perfect, −1 = opposite). Of the 19 pairs with a direction, 4 worked, 13 failed and 2 barely moved.

  • Main reason: new news after the forecast was written. The Australian central bank (RBA) hiked as expected but sounded much softer, so AUD — the model's strongest currency — became the weakest. New Zealand jobs data were weak (NZD fell). Swiss inflation was higher than expected and investors bought the franc as a safe haven, so CHF — scored −3 — became the strongest.
  • A fearful week: on Thursday US stocks fell 4.4%, and oil fell about 13% over the week. The model had a neutral risk mood when it was written.
  • What worked: GBP and CAD. GBPCAD, GBPNZD, USDCAD and CADJPY moved the right way.
  • The final check helped: cutting NZD from +3 to +1 made the result less bad (Phase 1 match -0.63, final -0.47).
  • Two weeks prove nothing. This was week 2 of 26. Nothing in the method changes until 26 weeks of results exist.

2 · How to read the results

Ranking match (ρ, "rho")
Compares the model's order of the 8 currencies with the order of their real moves. +1 = same order, 0 = no relation, −1 = opposite order.
σ ("sigma", normal move size)
How big a weekly move normally is. For now a fixed size is used: 1.2% for most pairs, 0.8% for EURGBP and EURCHF, 1.8% for GBPJPY (marked "uncalibrated" in the technical report). After 26 weeks of data, real sizes will be measured.
Worked / failed / barely moved
Worked = the pair moved in the forecast direction. Failed = it moved the other way. Barely moved = it moved less than a quarter of a normal move (0.25σ), so it does not count either way.
Why one week proves nothing
With 8 currencies, a good or bad week can easily be luck. The method needs about 26 weeks before its results mean anything. So far: week 1 match −0.12, week 2 match -0.47.

5 · Why the misses happened

Every big miss gets one code. Here is what each code means in plain words:

CodeMeaning
N1New news after the forecast (nobody could know it).
MMissed: the information existed before the forecast, but the research did not find it.
N2A known event was given too much or too little weight.
IInterpretation error: the facts were right, the reading was wrong.
PAlready priced: the market already expected it, or expectations moved the other way.
FMoney flows / positioning: traders buying or selling for reasons other than fundamentals.
XNoise: a small move, not worth explaining.
DData problem.
  • AUD → N1. RBA hike with a 6–3 vote and no promise of more hikes (3 Nov). Caused the misses in AUDUSD, EURAUD, AUDJPY, AUDCHF and AUDCAD.
  • CHF → N1 (+ F). Swiss inflation 1.5% vs 1.3% and safe-haven buying after the US elections and the stock fall. Caused the misses in USDCHF, GBPCHF, CHFJPY and part of AUDCHF/NZDCHF.
  • NZD → N1. Unemployment 5.7% vs 5.5% (4 Nov). Caused the EURNZD and NZDCHF misses.
  • EUR → N1 / P. Oil fell ~13% (good for the euro); the failed French vote was already expected.
  • No "M" this week: the research did not miss information that existed before the forecast (the one stale number, for NZD, had already been fixed in the final check).
  • Pattern to watch: a central bank meeting inside the forecast week has now surprised 3 times in 2 weeks (New Zealand, Canada, Australia). If it happens a 4th time within 12 weeks, a method change can be proposed.
  • Still unexplained: AUDCHF fell 1.25% on Monday, before the RBA meeting.

6 · Gold

Forecast: gold score +2 and dollar +2, so XAUUSD gap 0 → two-sided (no direction), not scored.

What happened: gold rose about +5.06% over the week, from a 1-year low. Most of it came on Monday (+1.84%) and Wednesday (+3.34%): a weaker dollar after the US elections, lower US interest rates and lower oil. It dipped a little on Friday after the strong US jobs report.

Why it matters: this time gold rose in a nervous week, unlike the two weeks before. Central banks bought a lot of gold in Q3 (321 tonnes) and gold funds had inflows in October. The intra-week update on 6 Nov already showed the gold factor much stronger than the frozen score.

9 · Lessons and patterns to watch

No rule changes until 26 weeks. These are only notes to check over time:

  • Big meetings inside the week are risky — now 3 times. Even when a hike is fully expected, the vote split and the message can surprise (RBA this week). Phase 1 should spell out that risk for every such meeting.
  • The risk mood can flip fast. At the forecast it was neutral, only 0.15 points from "afraid"; one bad day flipped it.
  • Oil swings fast. Oil was +11% over 4 weeks at the forecast, then fell ~13% in one week. The oil-based scores for CAD, EUR and JPY reversed inside the week.
  • Fresh market prices matter. The final check fixed an out-of-date NZD number and the result improved.
  • No warning triggered: no currency moved strongly against its forecast three weeks in a row (only two weeks of data so far).

11 · Glossary

Attribution code
A one-letter label explaining why a forecast missed (section 5).
Central bank
The institution that sets a country's interest rate.
Currency strength
A currency's average move against the other seven during the week.
ETF
A fund that holds gold; its buying or selling moves the gold price.
Final check
The short check before Monday's open that can correct a score for new news or a missed fact.
Hawkish / dovish
Hawkish = wants higher rates (usually good for the currency). Dovish = wants lower rates.
Hike / hold
Raise / keep the interest rate.
Priced / already priced
What the market already expects. If something is already priced, it usually moves the currency less.
Risk-on / risk-off
Risk-on = investors are confident and buy riskier assets. Risk-off = investors are afraid and buy safe havens.
Safe haven
An asset people buy when afraid: traditionally JPY, CHF and gold.
Two-sided
A pair with no clear fundamental direction; not scored.
Uncalibrated
Using fixed move sizes until enough real data exist.
Vote split
How the central-bank members voted (e.g. 6–3 = six for the decision, three against).
