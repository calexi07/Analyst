1 · The answer: did the forecast work?

Partly — the best week so far, but still weak. The ranking match was +0.18 (0 = no relation, +1 = perfect, −1 = opposite) — positive for the first time. Of the 22 currency pairs with a direction, 9 worked, 8 failed and 5 barely moved. Gold, called up, fell 4.7%.

  • Big win: the US dollar. It was the model's strongest currency and the strongest in real life. All 7 dollar pairs moved the right way, including the two most confident calls (EURUSD and GBPUSD down).
  • Big miss: the Swiss franc. Scored 0, it was the weakest currency of the week, so all pairs that expected the franc to hold up failed.
  • Gold: hot US inflation pushed interest rates up and gold down.
  • The final check helped again: the New Zealand correction lifted the match from +0.10 to +0.18.
  • Three weeks prove nothing. This was week 3 of 26. Nothing in the method changes until 26 weeks of results exist.

2 · How to read the results

Ranking match (ρ, "rho")
Compares the model's order of the 8 currencies with the order of their real moves. +1 = same order, 0 = no relation, −1 = opposite.
σ ("sigma", normal move size)
How big a weekly move normally is. For now a fixed size: 1.2% for most pairs, 0.8% for EURGBP and EURCHF, 1.8% for GBPJPY ("uncalibrated").
Worked / failed / barely moved
Worked = moved in the forecast direction. Failed = moved the other way. Barely moved = less than a quarter of a normal move (0.25σ); it does not count.
Why one week proves nothing
With 8 currencies, a good or bad week can be luck. About 26 weeks are needed. So far: week 1 match −0.12, week 2 −0.47, week 3 +0.18 (average −0.13).

5 · Why the misses happened

Every big miss gets one code:

CodeMeaning
N1New news after the forecast (nobody could know it).
MMissed: the information existed before, but the research did not find it.
N2A known event was given too much or too little weight.
IInterpretation error: right facts, wrong reading.
PAlready priced: the market already expected it.
FMoney flows: buying or selling for non-fundamental reasons.
XNoise: a small move.
DData problem.
  • CHF → N1 (+ F). Fear faded after the forecast and US rates were set to rise much more than Swiss ones; money that ran into the franc the week before ran out again. Caused the misses in EURCHF, GBPCHF, NZDCHF and CADCHF.
  • Gold → N1. Hot US inflation (10 Nov) and talk of a 0.50% Fed hike pushed US interest rates up (10-year near 5.1%). Note (I): the model expected a more hawkish Fed but did not let that lower gold's score.
  • NZD → N2. The election was known, but its meaning for the central bank was weighted too little.
  • CAD → N1. A hawkish Bank of Canada summary and Thursday's oil jump.
  • AUD → N1. Very weak Australian confidence surveys and markets betting the RBA is done.
  • No "M" this week: nothing that existed before the forecast was missed.
  • Pattern to watch: for the second week in a row, the market mood flipped right after the forecast (two weeks ago calm → fear; last week fear → calm). The franc was hurt both times by the mood part of its score. 2 times so far; a method change can only be proposed at 4 times within 12 weeks.

6 · Gold

Forecast: gold's own score +7, so XAUUSD was bullish, medium confidence (gap +7).

What happened: gold fell −4.71% over the week (a big move: 3.9 normal moves on the fixed scale). It rose +1.7% on Monday (calmer news, softer dollar), then fell on Tuesday (−2.6%, hot inflation) and Thursday (−2.8%), ending near $3,880–3,900.

Why: gold pays no interest, so when US interest rates jump it gets less attractive. The model's gold score leaned on fear (which faded) and on central-bank and fund buying, and did not include the rate risk the dollar part of the model was predicting. The daily checks already flagged this from Tuesday evening.

9 · Lessons and patterns to watch

No rule changes until 26 weeks. Notes only:

  • The mood part of the score is fragile. When fear is at its maximum on the forecast day, safe-haven scores (CHF, JPY) can flip fast.
  • Gold and the dollar share one story. If the model expects a more hawkish Fed, that is also bad news for gold.
  • Read elections by what they mean for the central bank, not only by seats.
  • The daily checks were useful for gold: from Tuesday evening they flagged gold as "invalidated", and gold kept falling (−3.8% Wed–Fri). They were wrong about the euro getting stronger. Still too few checks to judge (16 of 20 needed).
  • No warning triggered: no currency moved strongly against its forecast three weeks in a row.

11 · Glossary

Attribution code
A one-letter label explaining why a forecast missed (section 5).
Carry
Earning a currency's interest rate; high-rate currencies attract money.
Central bank
The institution that sets a country's interest rate.
Currency strength
A currency's average move against the other seven during the week.
Final check
The short check before Monday's open that can correct a score for new news or a missed fact.
Hawkish / dovish
Hawkish = wants higher rates (usually good for the currency). Dovish = wants lower rates.
Intermediary (daily) check
A check during the week of whether the forecast still holds; it never changes the score.
Priced
What the market already expects.
Risk-on / risk-off
Risk-on = investors are confident. Risk-off = investors are afraid and buy safe havens.
Safe haven
An asset people buy when afraid: traditionally JPY, CHF and gold.
Two-sided
A pair with no clear fundamental direction; not scored.
Uncalibrated
Using fixed move sizes until enough real data exist.
