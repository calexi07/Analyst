Week 47 — the currency story in plain words

Easy-read edition · PHASE 1 · Mon 16 – Fri 20 Nov 2026 · explains Forex_Fundamental_Bias_Engine_2026-11-14_Week47_PHASE1.html (the technical report is the analysis of record)

Fundamental analysis only, not a trade signal. No entries, stops or targets. The scores say which way the economic news leans, not when or how far a price will move.

1How to read a score

Each currency gets a score from −18 to +18. Above +3 means the news leans in its favour (bullish); below −3 means it leans against it (bearish); in between is neutral. For a pair like EURUSD we subtract the second score from the first. A difference (the gap) of 3 or more gives a lean; 2 or less is two-sided (no favourite). Conviction (high/medium/low) says how strong and consistent the case is. Confidence says how good the data behind it is.

2The week in short

  • Markets calmed down. Last week they were scared (risk mood −2); now they are neutral (0). Safe places to hide money (Swiss franc, yen, gold) lose that extra support, and higher-paying currencies (pound, Aussie, kiwi) recover.
  • The US dollar is still the strongest (+5): US prices rose faster than expected and Fed officials talk about more rate rises.
  • The Canadian dollar is the weakest (−4) because oil fell about 13% in four weeks and US tariffs are still in place.
  • Gold's own score turned negative (−3): US real interest rates rose and nobody is rushing to safety.
  • No big central-bank decision this week. The big tests are Canadian, British, Australian and Japanese data and the Fed minutes on Wednesday.

5The six ingredients

  • R1 Carry (15%): how much the currency pays in interest compared with the others.
  • R2 Repricing (35%): is the market likely to expect a tougher (hawkish) or softer (dovish) central bank soon?
  • R3 Surprises (10%): did important data beat or miss forecasts?
  • N1 Trade income (10%): prices of the country's key exports (oil for Canada, iron ore for Australia, dairy for New Zealand).
  • N2 Political and fiscal risk (15%): budgets, elections, tariffs, intervention threats.
  • G1 Risk mood (15%): calm markets help high-yield currencies, fear helps safe havens. This week it is 0 for everyone.

16Glossary

  • Bias: the direction the fundamentals lean.
  • Conviction: how strong and consistent the case is.
  • Confidence: how good the data behind a score is.
  • Hawkish / dovish: a central bank leaning to higher / lower interest rates.
  • Priced: already expected by traders.
  • Carry: the interest a currency pays.
  • Spread: the extra yield one bond pays over another.
  • Real yield: interest after inflation.
  • Breakeven: the inflation rate bond markets expect.
  • Gap: base score minus quote score.
  • Two-sided: no favourite.
  • ρ (rho): how well the ranking matched reality, from −1 to +1.
  • bp: basis point, 0.01 percentage point.
